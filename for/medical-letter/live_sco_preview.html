1. Finerenone (Kerendia) Renal/Cardiovascular Outcomes, Potassium Monitoring & CYP3A4 Contraindications

Finerenone is a nonsteroidal selective mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist approved to reduce sustained eGFR decline, end-stage kidney disease, cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, and heart-failure hospitalization in adults with CKD associated with type 2 diabetes receiving maximally tolerated ACE-inhibitor or ARB therapy. Do not initiate if serum potassium exceeds 5.0 mEq/L or eGFR is <25 mL/min/1.73 m2: start 10 mg once daily for eGFR 25 to <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 (or 20 mg once daily for eGFR >=60), recheck serum potassium at 4 weeks, withhold doses if K+ exceeds 5.5 mEq/L, and avoid concomitant strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (contraindicated).

2. Comparative Pharmacokinetics & Dose Conversion of Basal and Concentrated Insulins in Type 2 Diabetes

Second-generation ultra-long-acting basal insulins (insulin degludec U-100/U-200 and insulin glargine U-300) deliver flatter, peakless 24-to-42-hour exposure profiles and lower rates of nocturnal symptomatic hypoglycemia than NPH insulin or U-100 insulin glargine. When switching a patient from twice-daily NPH insulin to once-daily insulin glargine U-300, reduce the total daily NPH unit dose by 20% to prevent early hypoglycemia and adjust doses no more frequently than every 3 to 4 days once steady state is reached.
