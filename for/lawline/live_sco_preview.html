1. ABA Formal Opinion 512: Model Rule 1.1 Competence & Rule 1.6 Client Confidentiality

Under ABA Formal Opinion 512, attorneys need not be machine-learning engineers to satisfy Model Rule 1.1 technological competence, but must understand the capabilities, hallucination limitations, and data-retention terms of any generative AI tool used in practice. Inputting confidential client facts into a self-learning or provider-retained GAI tool without assessing disclosure risks - or obtaining informed client consent where client identity or strategy could be exposed - violates Model Rule 1.6.

2. Model Rule 3.3 Candor Toward the Tribunal, Rule 5.1/5.3 Supervision & Rule 1.5 Billing Ethics

Model Rule 3.3(a)(1) prohibits knowingly making a false statement of law or fact or failing to correct unverified, fabricated AI case citations submitted to a court. Managing lawyers must enforce firm-wide primary-source citation checks under Rules 5.1 and 5.3. Under Model Rule 1.5, hourly-billing lawyers may charge only for actual attorney/staff time spent prompting, reviewing, and verifying outputs - never phantom 'value hours' that an associate would have spent drafting manually without AI.
